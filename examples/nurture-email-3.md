# Example, nurture email 3

Status: DRAFT, written from the brain on 2026-10-19, pending founder approval.
Audience: an owner who took the scorecard and opted in. Stage: evaluation (day 5). Template: `../templates/nurture-email.md`.

---

**Subject:** We'll run it for you. Here's exactly how.
**Preview:** Four things from you, once. Everything else from us.

Hi [first name],

Your scorecard put [shop name] in the [tier] range. The gap behind that score is usually the same one: asking for reviews depends on someone remembering after a long day.

That's the part we run for you. Here's exactly how it works.

**What you do, once:**
1. Connect your job software.
2. Give us access to your Google profile.
3. Send us one team photo.
4. Approve your texting details.

**What we do, every week after that:**
- Once your software is connected, every customer gets a request after the job, with your team's photo and their first name. Then one reminder.
- Every new review gets a reply written for that review.
- You get an alert the day a negative review posts.
- Every month, you see what went out and what changed on your profile.

What we don't do: remove reviews, screen out unhappy customers, reward reviews, or promise you a number of reviews or a ranking. We ask everyone the same honest question, the way Google's rules require.

`[OFFER PENDING]` Price, setup fee, and guarantee go here once the offer is DECIDED.

If you'd like to see it running, the video takes a few minutes:

[Watch how it works]

Have three or more locations, or a question first? Book a 15-minute call: [link]

[founder name]
Rempo

[unsubscribe]

---

## Why this works
- It opens with the reader's own result, not with Rempo.
- "What you do once / what we do" makes done for you concrete and honest about the owner's part.
- The "what we don't do" paragraph answers the compliance worry before it is asked.
- One primary call to action, with the call link as a plain text line for those who need a person.
- The offer block is left pending instead of filled with prices that are not live.

## QA
Checked: opt-in audience, one idea, banned words, em dashes, claims (no promise, no proof points), status (no PLANNED item stated; no system named), glossary, unsubscribe present. Closest to breaking: "Every month, you see what went out and what changed" edges toward the baseline report, which is PLANNED; it describes the monthly report, which is live.
