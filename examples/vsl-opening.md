# Example, VSL opening (first 60 to 90 seconds)

Status: DRAFT, written from the brain on 2026-10-19, pending founder approval.
Audience: the owner. Stage: discovery. Template: `../templates/vsl-script.md`. Hook: A, done for you.
Spoken by the founder. Lines are written for the ear.

---

**[0:00]** Pull up your Google profile and look at your newest review.
[pause]
If it's from last month, or last season, you're not alone.

**[0:08]** It's not that your work got worse. It's that asking for reviews depends on someone remembering. After a long day. Between calls.

**[0:18]** And here's why that matters. Most people look for reviews from the last three months. Seventy-four percent, according to BrightLocal's 2026 survey.
[on screen: "74% look for reviews from the last three months. BrightLocal 2026"]

**[0:30]** They also look at whether you answer. Eighty percent say they'd rather use a business that replies to all its reviews.
[on screen: "80% prefer a business that replies to all reviews. BrightLocal 2026"]

**[0:40]** I'm [founder name], and at Rempo we run your Google reviews for you. Every job asked. Every review answered.

**[0:48]** Here's what that means. Once your job software is connected, every customer gets a request after the job, with your team's photo and their first name on it. Then one reminder. Nobody at your shop has to send anything.
[on screen: the personalized request on a phone, labeled "Sample"]

**[1:05]** When a review comes in, it gets a reply written for that review. Not a template.
[on screen: a reply to a sample review, labeled "Sample"]

**[1:12]** What do you do? You give us access once. Your job software, your Google profile, a team photo. That's it. We do the rest.

**[1:22]** In the next few minutes I'll show you exactly what we set up, what we don't do, and what it costs.

---

## Why this works
- The hook asks the owner to look at their own profile, so the problem is theirs, not ours.
- Both statistics are said and shown with the source, and neither is a promise.
- "Once your job software is connected" keeps the done-for-you claim conditional, as the claim ledger requires.
- The request and reply are shown, labeled as samples, with no claimed lift.
- It ends on what comes next, not on a price, because the offer is not DECIDED.

## QA
Checked: writing for the ear (read aloud, no line over one breath), banned words, em dashes, claims (no review, rating, or ranking promise; no proof points; no PLANNED item stated), sources on every statistic, samples labeled, one audience. Closest to breaking: "Nobody at your shop has to send anything" drifts toward the absolute "no buttons to click"; it holds only because the connection condition sits in the sentence before.
