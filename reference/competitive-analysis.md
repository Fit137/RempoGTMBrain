# Competitive analysis

Load for: market shape, who the buyer compares Rempo with, and where Rempo can lead. Distills: Phase 2, `dashboards/02-competitive-market-analysis.md`. Facts dated 2026-10; re-check before citing.

## The slate

A competitor earns a slot only if this buyer actually weighs it.

| Slot | Rival | Why it is on the slate |
|---|---|---|
| Closest self-serve substitute | NiceJob | Review software at $75 to $125/month, bought without a call. Sets the price a buyer expects for a tool |
| Already paid for | Jobber Reviews (and the Housecall Pro equivalent) | The ICP keeps jobs in this software; the review switch is already there |
| Category leader | Podium | The name owners know; sold by demo, reported at about $399 to $599/month on annual terms |
| Done-for-you alternative | Footbridge Media | Contractor marketing at $249/month with website, profile optimization, and review management; no contract; 90-day money-back guarantee |
| Status quo | Google Business Profile by hand | The free review link plus someone remembering to send it |

**Out of the slate:** Birdeye (multi-location brands, outside the ICP); GoHighLevel agencies (a market, not one competitor); Scorpion (sales-led agency, 12+ month contracts).

## What buyers say about the set (review analysis)

- **Praise:** tools that run without effort (NiceJob: easy setup, 36 G2 mentions; simple review process, 33), integrations, native workflow (Jobber), breadth (Podium).
- **Complaints:** setup effort and slow onboarding help; contracts that are hard to leave (Podium: difficult cancellation, 100% negative across 24 Capterra mentions); wording that feels generic; fixed follow-up.
- **Consumers agree:** 50% are put off by generic or templated replies (BrightLocal 2026).

## Category summary

- **Vector 1 leader:** NiceJob, with Jobber close behind for its own users. Wins self-serve buyers and sets the tool price at $75 to $125/month.
- **Vector 2 leader:** Podium among software; Footbridge among services. Breadth is a cost for a buyer who only wants reviews handled.
- **Vector 3 leader:** Rempo, by design. Brings the buyer's effort close to zero and justifies a price above the tools.
- **Vector 4 leader:** Rempo, by design. The one claim that shows in a single image and a single reply. Its effect on response rates is unproven, `{TBD}`.

## The wedge

Compete on effort, not features. The tools, from the free Google link to Podium, leave someone at the shop running the program. Footbridge runs the website and treats reviews as one bundled tool. Rempo runs the review program itself, from the jobs the shop already logs, and sells it the way the tools sell: a published price, bought online.

**The obvious-choice buyer:** the owner of a busy home-service shop that already runs jobs in Jobber, Housecall Pro, or similar, who wants reviews handled without another tool and without a contract. For that buyer, vectors 3 and 4 decide.

## Defensibility

Not in the features: any agency on GoHighLevel can buy them. It sits in the operating playbook (connections, request timing, reply quality, compliance with Google's April 2026 rules), a home-service focus that makes every result transfer to the next buyer, and the proof base the founding cohort builds. Until customers exist, all of it is designed, not proven.
