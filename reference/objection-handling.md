# Objection handling

Load for: the FAQ, sales calls, nurture emails, comparison pages, and ad comments. Distills: the blockers in Phases 4 and 5, the weaknesses in Phase 2, and the claim ledger. Synthesized.

Each heading is the objection in the buyer's words. The answer is honest: where the answer is "not yet", it says not yet. Never argue a buyer out of a fair point.

## "We already have reviews in Jobber."
That switch sends a request when a job closes, and that's useful. Someone at your shop still owns the replies, the follow-ups, and checking it works. We run the whole program: a request after every completed job, a reminder, a reply written for each review, and a monthly report. Whether we connect to your Jobber account at launch: we confirm before you buy (`{DEFERRED: product}`). We make sure a customer isn't asked twice (`{DEFERRED: product}`).

## "NiceJob is $75 a month. Why pay more?"
NiceJob is a good tool, and it's cheaper. You run it. Rempo is a service that runs it for you: setup, requests, replies, reporting. If you'd rather run it yourself, a tool is the right call.

## "What's the setup fee for?"
Our team connects your job software and Google profile, writes your messages, builds your request image with your team photo, and registers your texting number. It's real work done once. (Today's live fee: $299 on Starter. The refund-if-late guarantee is PLANNED; do not offer it until DECIDED.)

## "Will texts annoy my customers?"
Each customer gets one request after their job and one reminder. That's it. Texts go only to customers who gave their number with consent (wording `{TBD}` with counsel), and texting rules require an opt-out in every text.

## "Can you guarantee reviews, or a higher ranking?"
No, and anyone who does is promising something nobody controls. What we control, and what we report every month: every completed job asked, a reminder, every review answered. You see your numbers against where you started. Reviews are one of the things Google weighs in local results; we track your position and never promise it.

## "Can you remove a bad review?"
No. We don't remove reviews. You get an alert the day a negative review posts, and every review gets a calm reply written for it, so you can decide whether to call the customer first.

## "We tried this before and it didn't work."
Usually the tool was switched on once and nobody owned it, or the messages read like software. We run it every month and you see the work in the report. If the issue was something else, tell us on a 15-minute call and we'll be straight about whether we fit.

## "I don't want a contract."
Today's terms are on the live page. Month-to-month terms are part of the PLANNED offer: say "month to month" only once the decision log marks it DECIDED.

## "Is this allowed by Google?"
Yes, the way we do it. Every customer gets the same honest request. We never filter out unhappy customers, never offer anything for a review, never ask customers to mention a technician or suggest what to write. Those are Google's rules, including the ones added in April 2026, and the FTC's.

## "What do I have to do?"
Once: connect your job software, give us access to your Google profile, send a team photo, and confirm your texting details. After that, requests go out without anyone at your shop sending them.

## "We're on Podium."
If you use Podium's inbox, chat, and payments, it may be worth keeping. If you mainly want your reviews handled, you may be paying for the rest. If you're under contract, finish it first. Our price is on the page and you can buy without a demo.

## "I'd rather talk to someone."
Book a 15-minute call. You talk to the founder: questions, whether your software fits, and a quote if you have three or more locations.

Then "after every job" can't run without someone at your shop, so we'd rather talk first. Book a call; an upload option is `{DEFERRED: product}`.
