Load for: headlines, page sections, ad copy, and email bodies built on the pillars. Distills: Phase 6, `dashboards/06-value-proposition.md`. One audience: the owner buys. The office manager's line ("nothing new for you to do") belongs in onboarding, not ads.

Verbatim approved lines live in [`ops/copy-bank.md`](../ops/copy-bank.md). Use those first; this file explains what each one is allowed to carry.

## Brand promise and positioning

- **Brand promise:** Every customer asked, every review answered, and you never have to run it.
- **Positioning:** a done-for-you Google review program for home-service shops, run from the job software they already use. The point of difference is the combination: run for you, from your own job data, and personal rather than templated.

## Rules that travel with these lines

- Every "Get started" carries the offer line once the offer is DECIDED; until then label the draft `[OFFER PENDING]`.
- RTB 1.2 names a job-software system only when product confirms it; RTB 1.3 is PLANNED.
- RTB 2.2 context, not proof: 80% of consumers say they are more likely to use a business that replies to all its reviews (BrightLocal 2026). Cite it as a market fact.
- RTB 3.2: the website display never implies it shows every review; social posts quote reviewers by first name only and need approval (`{DEFERRED: product}`).
- RTB 2.1 claims no lift. The request-style test in the [90-day experiment](experiment-90-day.md) is the only route to a number.
- Body copy: 25 words or fewer per drill.

Pillar order on a page: VP 1, VP 2, VP 3. Positioning behind it: [positioning](positioning.md).
