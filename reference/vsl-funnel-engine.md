# VSL funnel engine (the channel engine)

Load for: any ad, VSL, page, email, or retargeting asset; planning, writing, or editing one. Distills: Phases 3, 7, 8, and 10 (`dashboards/07-customer-journey.md`, `08-assets-library.md`, `10-90-day-experiment.md`). Synthesized.

## Thesis

One promise, one picture, one checkout. Owners of busy home-service shops are mostly not searching for review help. They recognize the problem when they see it: their newest review is old, and asking depends on someone remembering. So the engine shows the problem and the fix in pictures (a phone receiving a personalized request; a reply written for a real-looking review) and sends every click to one VSL page where they can buy without a call.

## How the channels feed the one page

| Channel | Role | Sends people to |
|---|---|---|
| Meta ads (primary) | Creates demand with pictures; the request is the creative | VSL page, or the scorecard (lead form or link) |
| Google Search (secondary) | Captures owners already searching; teaches the buyer's words for the VSL and FAQ | Problem terms to the scorecard; solution terms to the VSL page; comparison pages |
| Nurture email (opt-ins only) | Four emails after the scorecard, then one useful email a month | VSL page and the call link, every email |
| Retargeting (Meta) | Second chance at every paid visit | VSL viewers who reached the offer; scorecard takers by tier |

The VSL page is the centre. Everything else is a way in or a way back.

## The three calls to action and the bumpers

1. **Primary: "Get started"** (checkout). Carries the offer line once DECIDED; until then drafts say `[OFFER PENDING]`.
2. **Bumper 1: "Book a 15-minute call"** for questions, no job software, or three or more locations.
3. **Bumper 2: "Not ready? Take the scorecard"** for everyone else, which puts them on the list.

Order is fixed: buy, call, scorecard. One primary per section. The same primary at the top and bottom of a page.

## Hook archetypes (test, do not assume)

| Hook | Line | Shows |
|---|---|---|
| A. Done for you | "We run your Google reviews from your job software." | Owner on a job site; phone in pocket stays there |
| B. The picture | "Here's what your customer sees." | A phone receiving the personalized request, labeled as a sample |
| C. The process | "Every review answered, every job asked." | A review and the reply written for it, samples |
| Scorecard | "Most customers look for reviews from the last three months. How do yours look?" (BrightLocal 2026) | The scorecard's first question |

Ask or describe; never assert a fact about the viewer.

## VSL beat structure (5 to 8 minutes, the founder on camera)

1. **Hook (first 15 seconds):** the newest review is old, and asking depends on memory.
2. **Why it matters:** what buyers read: recency and replies (BrightLocal 2026, on screen).
3. **What Rempo runs:** every completed job asked, a reminder, a reply for every review, the monthly report.
4. **The picture:** the sample request and a sample reply.
5. **What you do once, what we do:** three steps for the owner; the rest is ours.
6. **Offer:** price, setup fee, guarantee on one screen (PLANNED until DECIDED).
7. **Three calls to action,** in order.

Never shown working: anything PARTIAL, PLANNED, or HELD. Captions and chapters on.

## Plan, write, edit

- **Plan:** pick one stage, one hook, one takeaway sentence, one CTA. If you cannot write the takeaway in a sentence, stop.
- **Write:** start from [`ops/copy-bank.md`](../ops/copy-bank.md). For anything spoken, write for the ear ([`rules/writing-rules.md`](../rules/writing-rules.md)). Templates: [`templates/README.md`](../templates/README.md).
- **Edit:** cut the warm-up; front-load the point; check every claim against [`rules/do-not-say.md`](../rules/do-not-say.md) and every capability against [`rules/feature-status.md`](../rules/feature-status.md); confirm one CTA; run [`ops/QA-checklist.md`](../ops/QA-checklist.md).

## Creative cadence

One lead message with three hooks in two formats (short video, static), refreshed about every four weeks: about 18 assets over 90 days. The founder records; a freelancer edits; the research strategist writes hooks, the VSL outline, and the FAQ.

## What good looks like (measured, not assumed)

| Step | Watch |
|---|---|
| Ads | CTR; cost per VSL page visit; cost per purchase by hook |
| VSL page | Play rate; share reaching the offer; clicks per CTA |
| Checkout | Start to purchase; qualifier failures; bump and upsell take |
| Bumpers | Calls booked, held, converted; scorecard opt-ins to purchase; self-serve share |
| After purchase | Onboarding booked within 24 hours; days to first request |

Judge an ad set only after about $700 of spend (twice the CAC hurdle). Gates and bands: [90-day experiment](experiment-90-day.md). Assets and owners: [marketing campaigns](marketing-campaigns.md).
