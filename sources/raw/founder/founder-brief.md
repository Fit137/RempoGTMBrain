# Rempo — GTM Strategy Consultation Brief

**Website:** https://rempo.io  
**Business stage:** Early-stage, bootstrapped  
**GTM testing budget:** $15,000

## 1. Business & Offer

I'm building Rempo, a done-for-you reputation management service that helps businesses consistently generate Google reviews without learning software, manually following up, or relying on staff to remember to ask.

The service includes automated SMS/email review requests, follow-ups, AI review replies, review monitoring, and repurposing reviews into social media content. Considering adding video testimonial request (not a feature yet)

One differentiating feature is hyper-personalized review requests**:** Instead of generic messages, I use a real photo of the business owner or team with the customer's name dynamically inserted into the image. This makes requests feel more personal / human to increase the conversion from request to review.

I'm considering $97–$149/month, with website and Google Business Profile management as potential upsells. Pricing and packaging are not finalized.  
Review system : One time setup then it runs via automation.  
GBP management: One time setup then it runs via automation.  
Website : one time buildout then minor occasional update

Tech we use. GHL, Zapier, Neftyimage, and WP

## 2. Target Market & ICP

I'm undecided between:

- **Broad local service businesses:** Larger market, but potentially generic messaging.

- **Home-service businesses:** More focused, including contractors, plumbers, roofers, etc, but Highly targeted

- **One specific niche:** Stronger specialization and differentiation, but potentially smaller demand/ TAM.

I'm also considering **marketing managers and office managers** as an ICP or champion instead of business owners. These roles may be underserved by advertising, experience the operational pain directly, and typically work for more established businesses with potentially greater purchasing capacity, rather than solo operators. However, their purchasing authority is uncertain.

## 3. Positioning & Differentiation

Reputation management is a crowded, sophisticated market dominated by established companies such as Podium, Birdeye, and NiceJob. Additionally many CRM have their own plugin

I'm exploring three positioning angles:

1.  **Better review conversion:** Hyper-personalized visual requests instead of generic automation.

2.  **Done-for-you autopilot:** No software to learn, no buttons to click, no manual follow-ups, and no relying on employees to remember. Rempo handles setup and automation then it runs on autopilot.

3.  **Frictionless purchase:** Transparent pricing, no mandatory 45-minute sales pitch, and no expensive agency retainer.

I'm unsure which message should lead, whether combining them creates a stronger offer, or whether I need a different positioning strategy entirely.

## 4. Sales Model

**a/ Sales approach.  
**I'm considering two main approaches:

**Traditional sales-led (NOT MY FAVORITE):** Ad → Landing page → Book a sales call → Subscribe → Onboarding → Setup & Activation.

**Direct purchase (MY FAVORITE):** Ad → Landing page → Subscribe → Book onboarding → Setup & Activation.

My hypothesis is that cold B2B prospects may be willing to purchase a $97–$149/month done-for-you service without a sales call, and might be their preferred way of purchase giving that the outcome is simple (more reviews). In this case “done for you” should be clearly communicated.

**b/ Execution.  
**I personally conduct onboarding and discuss potential upsells. My technical assistant handles the actual setup and implementation.

I'm also open to a hybrid model where prospects can purchase directly or optionally book a consultation.

## 5. Acquisition, Team & Budget

I'm considering Meta advertising but haven't committed to it as the primary channel.

I'm evaluating two hiring options:

- **Media buyer + research strategist — $900/month:** Market research, positioning, messaging, scripts, campaign management, and optimization. I own creative production

- **Media buyer + creative agency — $2,400/month combined:** Adds research and finished video/static creative production.

My concern is whether the additional creative production expense is justified with a modest media budget.

I'm also considering Google Search Ads, partnerships/referrals.

**SEO is another possibility at approximately $500/month**, however, the market is very established and it might be very difficult to organically rank for the main keywords.  
It can work well, particularly if I choose a narrow niche where organic competition may be more manageable. I haven't hired an SEO provider yet.

My total initial budget is **$15,000**, including external support and advertising. I work full-time, so I need delegated execution, although I handle customer onboarding and upsells.

My working CAC assumption is $350, but I have not validated it.

## 6. What I'd Like Your Help With

I'd like to use our consultation to make five key decisions:

1.  **ICP:** Broad local services, home services, or a specific niche? Owner versus office/marketing manager? Or a completely different ICP?

2.  **Positioning:** How can Rempo stand out in a mature market? Is personalization, done-for-you convenience, or frictionless purchasing the strongest angle? Or something else I'm not thinking of?

3.  **Offer and sales model:** Is $97–$149 appropriate? Can direct purchase followed by onboarding work, or should I use sales calls?

4.  **Acquisition:** Which channel should I prioritize, and do I need both a media buyer and a creative agency?

5.  **Execution:** How would you allocate $15,000 across a 90-day GTM experiment, with clear success and stop criteria?

These are my questions, and they are based on my own research and hypothesis. However, I may be missing other important factors or analytics that you would want to bring up.

**My main objective:** Identify a differentiated offer, the right customer, and a repeatable acquisition model that can scale.

I'm sharing this ahead of our meeting per your recommendation so we can spend the call discussing strategic decisions rather than covering the business background. What I shared is my own hypothesis based on market observation doing the best I could, but I'm open to different feedback and strategy.
